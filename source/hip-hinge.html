The Hip Hinge

Side view, facing right. Your hips move back; your back stays flat; your knees bend only a little. This is the base of deadlifts and swings.

1 · Start

Feet hip-width. Knees soft (small bend). Hands on the creases at the front of your hips.

2 · Hinge ✓

Push hips straight BACK. Your chest tips forward as a result. Back flat, neck long, eyes on the floor ~2 m ahead. Shins stay almost vertical.

✗ Rounded back

Bending at the waist and curling the spine. Hips stay over the feet. This puts the load on your lower back.

✗ Squatting it

Knees push forward and hips drop down instead of back. That is a squat, not a hinge.
How to do one rep
  1. Stand tall, feet hip-width, knees slightly bent. Keep that small bend the whole time.
  2. Put your hands in the creases at the front of your hips.
  3. Push your hips straight back, like you are closing a car door behind you with your butt.
  4. Let your chest tip forward. Keep your back flat and your chin tucked.
  5. Stop when you feel a strong stretch in the back of your thighs (hamstrings). That is usually when your hands slide to just above your knees.
  6. Drive your hips forward and squeeze your glutes to stand tall. Do not lean back at the top.
Two drills to learn it Wall drill: Stand with your back to a wall, heels about one foot (30 cm) away. Hinge until your butt touches the wall. Take a small step forward and repeat. The farther away you get, the more you hinge.

Broomstick drill: Hold a broomstick along your spine: one hand behind your neck, one hand behind your lower back. Hinge. The stick must stay in contact with your head, upper back, and tailbone. If it loses contact, your back is rounding.
